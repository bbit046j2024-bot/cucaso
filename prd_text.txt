1. Overview
1.1 Purpose of this document
This PRD defines what the CUCASO platform must do, for whom, and how success will be measured. It is the shared reference for the Administration Council, the organization's officers and the development team, and it is intended to be reviewed and signed off before design and development begin.
1.2 Background
Seventh-day Adventist universities, colleges and schools along the Mombasa coast - both public and private - participate through their SDA chapters. Today, coordination of joint rallies relies on phone calls, messaging groups, spreadsheets and cash handling. This makes it hard to know who is attending, what the event will cost, what each institution should contribute, and whether the money collected will be enough.
1.3 Problem statement
The organization has no single, clear public presence that explains 
who it is and why it exists
, and that welcomes new institutions.
Attendee data arrives in inconsistent formats from many institutions, with duplicates and gaps.
Rally costs are calculated manually, and fees are agreed case by case, with little transparency.
Leaders cannot see early enough whether funds will cover the attending population.
Approvals, payments and decisions are not recorded in one place, so accountability is weak.
1.4 Product vision
One trusted home for the coastal Adventist education family: a welcoming public website that unites and informs, and a secure portal where chapters register, submit attendees, receive a fair capability-based fee, pay into one account, and where each leader sees exactly what they need to lead.

2. Goals, Non-goals and Success Metrics
2.1 Goals
Present the organization clearly to its audiences, with a compelling explanation of its purpose and unity.
Provide one source of truth for chapters, attendees, fees and payments.
Calculate each chapter's fee automatically, transparently and according to capability.
Give early warning when funds will not cover the attending population.
Give each leader role-appropriate control and visibility, with a full audit trail.
Make joining simple for new chapters, and make the platform reusable for every recurring rally and venue.
2.2 Non-goals (version 1)
A full student information system or learning platform.
Native mobile apps (a mobile-first web app, optionally installable, is sufficient).
Individual student payments and card payments (chapters remit a single payment).
Managing chapters' internal finances (an optional collection tracker is a later addition).

6. Business Rules and Cost Calculation
6.1 Budget
Total budget = (Sum of fixed costs
              + Sum of per-head rates x total registered attendees
              + Sum of per-vehicle costs) x (1 + contingency %)
6.2 Capability-weighted fee
Chapter fee = Total budget x (chapter weight / sum of weights of all participating chapters)
Weights come from the chapter's tier, snapshotted for the rally. The fees of all chapters always add up exactly to the budget. Fees recalculate as chapters join or leave, headcount changes or cost items change, until the fee lock date; after locking, changes are handled through supplementary invoices or credit notes. Other allocation modes (equal, headcount-weighted, base plus per head) are selectable per rally.
Fee adjustments
When the Council approves a subsidy or waiver, the reduction is either (a) 
redistributed
 across the remaining chapters in proportion to their weights (default), or (b) 
covered by a named external source
 such as a sponsor or reserve fund. The chosen treatment is recorded with the adjustment.
6.3 Sufficiency check
Funded:
 fees invoiced (after adjustments and external funding) equal or exceed the budget and collected payments equal or exceed the budget.
On track:
 invoiced fees cover the budget, the payment deadline has not passed, and collections meet the configured schedule.
Shortfall:
 collections or projected collections fall below the configured threshold, or the deadline has passed with less than the full budget collected. The dashboard shows the exact gap and which chapters are outstanding.
Per-head coverage:
 funds collected divided by registered attendees, compared with the per-head cost.
6.4 Worked example
Fixed costs KES 200,000; per-head costs KES 800 x 500 attendees = KES 400,000; contingency 10%. Total budget = (200,000 + 400,000) x 1.10 = 
KES 660,000
. Cost to serve is the budget shared by headcount; cross-subsidy = fee minus cost to serve (positive: the chapter supports others; negative: the chapter is supported).

7. Key Workflows
7.1 A chapter joins
The chapter submits the application with officers and endorsement document.
The application enters the Council queue; members review, comment and vote.
On approval the Council assigns a tier; the system creates the chapter code and accounts and notifies the chapter.
The chapter appears in the member directory and can take part in the next rally.
7.2 A rally is set up
The Secretary creates the rally (or clones the last one), choosing the venue, deadlines and capacity.
The Central Treasurer sets cost items, contingency and allocation mode.
The Council confirms tiers and any adjustments; the rally opens for registration and chapters are notified.
7.3 Registration and fees
Chapter Reps upload attendees; validation catches errors and duplicates; consent is recorded for minors.
The engine updates the budget and each chapter's fee as headcount changes.
On the lock date, fees freeze and final invoices are issued with payment references.
7.4 Payment and reconciliation
Each chapter collects from its own members offline, then remits one payment to the central account using its reference.
Daraja callbacks (or manual entries) are matched to the chapter; balances and statuses update; a receipt is sent.
Unmatched payments are resolved by the Central Treasurer from the exceptions queue.
7.5 Shortfall handling
The dashboard flags a shortfall and shows the gap and outstanding chapters.
Reminders go to chapters; leaders review the options panel (fee change, attendance cap, subsidy or sponsorship).
Any decision is recorded with reasons and reflected in supplementary invoices where needed.
7.6 Rally close
Final attendance and logistics reports are produced; budget vs actual and reconciliation reports are reviewed.
The rally is archived, and its data feeds the rally history for future planning.

9. Non-Functional Requirements
Area
Requirement
Performance
Home page loads in under 3 seconds on a typical mobile connection; supports at least 100 chapters and 2,000 attendees per rally; a 500-row upload validates in under 30 seconds.
Availability
At least 99.5% availability during registration and payment windows; daily backups with periodic restore tests.
Security
HTTPS everywhere; hashed passwords; 2FA for admin roles; server-side role enforcement; rate limiting; input validation; secure file uploads; secrets kept out of code; validation of Daraja callbacks.
Privacy and legal
Compliance with the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019: lawful basis and consent, purpose limitation, data minimisation, guardian consent for minors, clear privacy notice, role-limited access, retention schedule, and handling of access and deletion requests. Confirm any registration obligations with legal counsel.
Auditability
All financial and governance actions are logged and cannot be edited by ordinary users.
Accessibility and usability
Mobile-first; WCAG 2.1 AA; simple language; works on low-end phones and low data; clear error messages.
Localisation
English first; optional Kiswahili; currency KES; East Africa Time; local phone number formats.
Maintainability
Automated tests for the cost engine using the worked example; separate test and production environments; documented configuration; error monitoring and logs.

12. Risks and Mitigations
Risk
Impact
Mitigation
Poor quality attendee data from bulk uploads
High
Template, strict validation, duplicate detection and an error report before saving.
Privacy breach involving minors' data
High
Guardian consent, data minimisation, role-limited access, encryption in transit, retention limits, Data Protection Act review.
Payments made outside the system or with wrong references
High
Clear invoice reference, unmatched-payment queue, manual entry with proof, reconciliation report.
Disputes about tiers and fees
Medium
Council-owned decisions, recorded reasons, audit trail, reconsideration request process, transparency to each chapter about its own fee.
Delays in Daraja onboarding or approvals
Medium
Start early; keep manual payment entry as a fallback from day one.
Low digital confidence among some users
Medium
Simple interface, short guides and videos, SMS support, training for chapter officers.
Changing cost rules between rallies
Medium
Configurable cost items, allocation modes and cloning; tests on the cost engine.
Loss of access to admin accounts
Medium
Multiple admins, 2FA recovery process, documented handover.
Scope creep
Medium
MoSCoW prioritization and phased releases.
13. Assumptions and Open Questions
13.1 Assumptions
One central collection account (Paybill, till or bank) receives all remittances.
The Council approval rule is a configurable majority vote.
Each institution has one chapter unless stated otherwise.
The Super Admin can configure the system but cannot silently override Council decisions; overrides, if permitted, are logged.

14. Glossary
Term
Meaning
Chapter
The SDA chapter of an institution; the unit that registers, is billed and pays.
Administration Council
The governing body that approves chapters, assigns tiers and approves fee adjustments.
Rally
A recurring joint gathering of member chapters, held at a chosen venue.
Tier / weight
A capability category and its numeric factor used to share the rally budget.
Fee lock
The moment fees freeze and final invoices are issued.
Remittance
The single payment a chapter makes to the central account.
Reconciliation
Matching received payments to invoices and to the account statement.
Daraja
Safaricom's API for M-Pesa integration.
Cross-subsidy
The difference between a chapter's fee and the cost of serving its attendees.
